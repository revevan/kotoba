Kotoba vs Pimsleur: course or trainer?

Pimsleur is a course: scripted 30-minute audio lessons that walk everyone down the same path, ~$20/month. Kotoba is a trainer: an adaptive vocabulary system that quizzes you, hears your answers, and reschedules every word around your memory — free. Both are hands-free. Choose by what you need: guided conversation practice → Pimsleur; vocabulary that sticks → Kotoba.

Disclosure: written by the developer of Kotoba. We've kept the Pimsleur half fair — it earned its reputation.

The core difference in one drive

On a Pimsleur drive, a narrator introduces a dialogue, prompts you — "how do you say, 'excuse me, where is the station?'" — pauses, and a native speaker gives the answer. It's active, well-paced, and completely fixed: the lesson neither knows nor cares what you said in the pause. On a Kotoba drive, there is no script. The session is assembled from your words: new ones taught with a slow breakdown and a native sentence, due ones quizzed. When you answer, speech recognition checks it; the result decides when that word returns. One is a great lecture; the other is a workout with a coach who's listening.

What each does better

  • Pimsleur: conversational patterns, polite-form grammar absorbed in context, pronunciation modeling from full native dialogues, a structured zero-to-basics path. If you want to be led, it leads well.
  • Kotoba: vocabulary volume (~3,000 JLPT N5–N3 words vs a few hundred phrases across Pimsleur's levels), verified recall on every rep, per-word spaced repetition, voice commands, browser-based with no subscription. If your bottleneck is words — and past the first month it usually is — the trainer wins.

Frequently asked questions

Does Kotoba teach grammar like Pimsleur does?

No. Every word arrives inside a natural native sentence, so patterns seep in by ear, but there's no explicit grammar instruction. Pair it with Language Transfer (free) or a textbook for grammar.

Is Pimsleur's "graduated interval recall" the same as spaced repetition?

It's the same insight, frozen at authoring time: intervals are baked into the script and identical for every learner. An SRS like FSRS computes intervals per word, per person, from actual recall history — which is only possible because Kotoba registers whether you got each word right.

I finished Pimsleur Level 1 — is Kotoba a good next step?

Very. That's exactly the point where scripted phrases stop scaling and vocabulary volume becomes the bottleneck. Start with the full N5 deck; the early words will fly by and the scheduler will find your edge quickly.
