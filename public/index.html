How it works

Three beats, all by ear.

1 · It teaches

A slow, spoken breakdown of each new word, then the word in a natural sentence.

“apple … in Japanese … りんご … riiin—gooo. For example: 毎朝りんごを食べます”

2 · You say it back

Speak it out loud. Kotoba listens with cloud speech recognition and confirms you got it.

Say りんご and it knows. If it mishears, you self-grade — by voice.

3 · It brings it back

Each word returns right before you’d forget it, quizzed and graded entirely by ear.

Modern spaced repetition (FSRS) — no streaks, no gems, no guilt.

Repetition that’s earned, not gamified

The scheduler adapts to how well you actually know each word and brings it back at the moment you’d start to forget. No streak-shaming, no XP — just words that quietly stick.

Words in real sentences

Every new word arrives inside a natural example sentence. Once a word matures, you hear the sentence with a beep where the word goes — and say the missing word. Context, still 100% by ear.

Questions

Asked and answered.

Can you really learn Japanese while driving?

Yes — but not by listening alone. Retention comes from active recall, so Kotoba asks you for the word, hears your answer, and grades it: every rep is a real memory test. It’s designed so your eyes never leave the road — after tapping Start once, the whole session, including “repeat”, “skip”, and self-grading, runs by voice.

How is this different from Pimsleur or a podcast?

Audio courses and podcasts play the same fixed lessons to everyone. Kotoba is interactive: it tracks which words you know, quizzes each one right before you’d forget it (FSRS spaced repetition), and actually hears whether you said it correctly. Think audio flashcards, not a radio show. It’s also completely free.

Is this like using Anki while driving?

That’s the idea — spaced-repetition flashcards you can run entirely hands-free. Anki has no audio-only or background mode, so it can’t be used safely in the car. Kotoba speaks each prompt, listens for your spoken answer, grades it with speech recognition, and schedules reviews the same way an SRS deck would.

Is Kotoba free?

Yes. Kotoba is free and open source — no subscription, no ads, no app store. It runs in your browser as a PWA; your progress lives on your device, with optional passwordless email sign-in if you want to sync across devices.

What vocabulary will I learn?

The JLPT lists: a curated N5 starter deck, full N5 (~670 words), N4 (~600), and N3 (1,640) — nearly 3,000 words with over 40,000 native-quality audio clips, each word taught inside a natural example sentence.

Does it work without a signal?

Sessions keep working offline once audio is cached — cell dead zones included. Speech recognition needs a connection, so when it’s unavailable Kotoba degrades gracefully to reveal-and-tap self-grading instead of stalling.
