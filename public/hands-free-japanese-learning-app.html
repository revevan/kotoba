The hands-free Japanese learning app.

"Hands-free mode" should mean three things at once: the app speaks every prompt, hears your spoken answer, and takes voice commands — so after one tap on Start you never touch or look at your phone. Kotoba is built that way from the ground up: a free, voice-controlled Japanese vocabulary trainer with speech-recognition grading and spaced repetition, for driving, chores, walks, and every other hour your hands are busy.

Why "hands-free" usually isn't

Ask for a Japanese app with a hands-free mode and you'll mostly be pointed at passive audio — Pimsleur, podcasts, audio courses. Those are hands-free in the sense that a radio is: sound comes out, nothing goes in. The moment an app needs to know whether you got the word right, it reaches for the screen — a tap to flip, a button to grade, a written answer. That's why learners keep asking forums for an app with a real hands-free mode and coming up empty: apps designed around a screen can't retrofit their exercises to voice. Hands-free has to be the starting constraint, not a feature toggle.

What a session sounds like

Kotoba: "apple … in Japanese … りんご … riiin—gooo. For example: 毎朝りんごを食べます。"
Kotoba, days later: "apple … your answer?"
You: 「りんご」
Kotoba: "✓ That's it." …and the scheduler pushes the word further out.

Everything in that loop is audio. New words get a slow spoken breakdown and a native example sentence; review words get quizzed right before you'd forget them (FSRS spaced repetition — the same modern algorithm Anki uses); your spoken answer is graded by cloud speech recognition. Mid-session, "repeat", "skip", "pause", and "got it" are all voice commands. As words mature, the difficulty climbs: you hear the example sentence with a beep where the word belongs and supply it yourself.

Built for the hours your eyes are busy

  • Driving — dash-mount or pocket, audio through car Bluetooth, the hands-free mic carries your answers. Sessions are easy to ignore when traffic needs you.
  • AirPods anywhere — chores, walks, the gym. The phone stays in your pocket.
  • Dead zones — sessions keep working offline once audio is cached; if speech recognition loses signal it degrades to spoken self-grading instead of stalling.
  • No app store — it's a web app (PWA). Open the site, tap Start.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Japanese app with a true hands-free mode?

Yes — Kotoba runs the entire session by voice: spoken prompts, speech-recognized answers, and voice commands. One tap on Start is the only touch.

Does Duolingo have a hands-free mode?

No. Its exercises are built around tapping and reading; even the audio lessons need the screen for answers.

What if the app mishears me?

Japanese speech recognition isn't perfect — accents, road noise, and homophones happen. When Kotoba isn't sure, it tells you the expected answer and lets you self-grade by voice ("got it" / "missed it") rather than marking you wrong on a technicality.

Is it really free?

Yes — free and open source, no ads, no subscription. Progress lives on your device with optional passwordless email sync.
