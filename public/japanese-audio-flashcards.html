Japanese audio flashcards: drill vocabulary entirely by ear.

Audio flashcards are spaced-repetition flashcards you review without a screen: the app speaks the prompt, you answer out loud, and it grades you and schedules the next review. That’s different from vocab playlists or podcasts, which never test you. Kotoba is a free web app built around exactly this loop for Japanese — JLPT N5–N3, native audio, hands-free from the first word to the last.

What are audio flashcards?

A normal flashcard asks your eyes a question; an audio flashcard asks your ears. The whole review loop happens in sound: a spoken prompt (“apple… in Japanese?”), a pause while you recall and say the answer — りんご — then confirmation and an example sentence. Grading and scheduling work like any SRS deck: words you know retreat into the future, words you fumble come back soon. The point is that active recall survives — you’re still being tested on every card — while your eyes and hands stay free for the road, the dishes, or the dog.

“Can’t I just listen to a vocab playlist?”

This is what most people find first: JLPT word-list albums on Spotify and YouTube, exported Anki deck audio, “learn Japanese while you sleep” compilations. They all share the same problem — they never ask you anything. Every word plays in a fixed order on a fixed schedule whether you’ve known it for months or never learned it. Recognition (“oh, I’ve heard that”) builds quickly; recall (producing the word yourself) doesn’t. Decades of testing-effect research say the quiz is the learning. A playlist is a radio show about your flashcards, not flashcards.

Kotoba is the audio-flashcard loop, built as its own product rather than bolted onto a visual app. Tap Start once and the session runs itself: new words get a slow spoken breakdown and a natural example sentence; review words get quizzed right before you’d forget them (FSRS scheduling — the same modern algorithm Anki offers); your spoken answers are graded by cloud speech recognition, and “repeat”, “skip”, and “pause” are voice commands. As words mature, the difficulty climbs: eventually you hear the example sentence with a beep where the word belongs and supply it yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Can you listen to flashcards like a playlist?

You can export Anki card audio or play JLPT albums, but that’s passive listening — nothing tests you, and every word repeats whether you know it or not. If you want the playlist experience with actual learning happening, that’s exactly the gap audio flashcards fill.

Are there audio flashcards for JLPT N5?

Yes — Kotoba’s N5 starter deck (~120 essential words) and full N5 deck (~670 words) are the recommended starting point, followed by N4 and N3. Every word comes with native audio and an example sentence.

Do audio flashcards work for kanji?

No — kanji recognition needs your eyes. Audio flashcards train listening, recall, and pronunciation. The pairing that works: a visual tool (Anki, WaniKani) at home, audio flashcards for the hours your eyes are busy.

Does it work with AirPods and car audio?

Yes — that’s the primary use case. Any Bluetooth audio works; the mic on AirPods or your car’s hands-free system carries your answers. Sessions keep working offline once audio is cached, and when speech recognition has no signal Kotoba falls back to spoken self-grading.

Is it really free?

Yes — free and open source. No ads, no premium tier, no app store. Optional passwordless email sign-in syncs progress across devices.
