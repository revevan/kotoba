Anki while driving? Here’s the audio-only alternative.

You can’t use Anki while driving — it has no audio-only mode, no background playback, and no voice input, so every card needs your eyes and thumbs. If you want real spaced repetition in the car, you need an app built to run by voice. For Japanese vocabulary, that app exists and it’s free: Kotoba speaks each prompt, hears your answer, grades it, and schedules the next review — hands-free from start to finish.

Can you use Anki while driving?

Not really, and it’s not for lack of people trying. Anki reviews are built around a loop of read the card → recall → flip → tap a grade. Every step assumes a screen and a free hand. Lock your phone or switch apps and the session simply stops — there is no background mode. The requests are easy to find and years old: an open Anki forum thread asking for background playback with the screen off, another asking to play card audio like a playlist, and an AnkiDroid issue requesting voice-control support that has been open since roughly 2019. None have shipped.

The workarounds people try

Each of these comes up in the forums, and each gives up the thing that makes Anki work:

  • Exporting card audio to a playlist. You get sound, but it plays every card in a fixed order whether you know the word or not. No recall attempt, no grading, no scheduling — it’s a podcast with extra steps.
  • Text-to-speech add-ons. The add-ons that attempted eyes-free review are abandoned, and even when they worked they still needed a tap to grade and flip each card.
  • AnkiDroid gesture taps. Blind-tapping regions of a screen you’re not supposed to be looking at, while driving, defeats the purpose entirely.
  • “I just do my reps at red lights.” This one shows up more often than it should. Please don’t.

The common thread: Anki’s scheduling is excellent, but its interface is fundamentally visual. Retrofitting audio onto it loses the active recall that makes spaced repetition worth doing.

What a hands-free SRS actually needs

To keep the memory science and lose the screen, the whole review loop has to move to audio. That means four things: the app speaks the prompt (“apple… in Japanese?”), it listens to your spoken answer, it grades what it heard with speech recognition rather than asking you to tap, and it schedules the next review with a real SRS algorithm. Mid-session controls — repeat, skip, pause — have to be voice commands too. That’s a different product, not an Anki add-on.

Frequently asked questions

Does Anki have a background or audio-only mode?

No. Anki stops when the screen locks or the app is backgrounded, on both desktop and mobile. Background playback and audio-only review are long-standing open feature requests, and the community add-ons that attempted screen-free review are abandoned.

Can AnkiDroid read cards aloud while I drive?

AnkiDroid can speak card text with TTS, but it still requires the screen on and a tap to flip and grade every card. The issue asking for true voice control has been open since ~2019.

Is it safe to do flashcards while driving?

Only if the session never asks for your eyes or hands — the moment an app needs a glance or a tap, it’s a distraction. A safe setup behaves like a phone call: everything through the speakers, everything answered by voice, and easy to ignore when traffic needs your full attention. That constraint is the entire design brief behind Kotoba.

What if I’m not learning Japanese?

Kotoba won’t help you yet — it’s Japanese-only. For other languages the current options are passive: audio courses like Pimsleur or Language Transfer, or exporting your deck’s audio to a playlist. Neither quizzes you nor schedules reviews, which is the gap this page is about.

Will this replace my Anki deck?

If your deck is Japanese vocabulary, it can — same spaced-repetition principle, FSRS scheduling, JLPT word lists. If your deck is kanji recognition, grammar notes, or another subject, keep Anki for home study and use Kotoba for the hours your eyes are busy. See also: Japanese audio flashcards, explained.
